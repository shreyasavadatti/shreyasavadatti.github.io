I'm a master's student at Carnegie Mellon University and a Quantum Research Assistant in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering. My research is in quantum error correction, quantum information theory, and quantum security.

The question I keep coming back to is how you can trust a computer built entirely from noisy parts. I like problems where the answer can be proved, so a lot of my work ends in a theorem or a certificate: what an error-corrected memory can and cannot do, or how much a set of noisy measurements really tells you about a gate. I also run my ideas on real hardware, and some of my favorite results are the ones where the device disagreed with me and I had to work out why.

I'm especially drawn to finding where quantum methods truly outperform the strongest classical ones. A quantum advantage only counts if it survives the best classical competitor, so I look for separations that can be proved, like a quantum memory that beats every adaptive classical strategy for reading out error-correction data.

I'm always glad to talk about research.

Certified Separation Between Coherent and Adaptive Temporal Processing of Quantum-Error-Correction Readout

Shreya Savadatti. arXiv preprint.

I study whether keeping quantum information between rounds of syndrome readout can improve the final error-correction decision. I bound every separate adaptive receiver, including receivers with unrestricted classical history and feed-forward, using a semidefinite relaxation, and compare that bound with an explicit coherent receiver. In a two-round benchmark, all separate adaptive receivers have recovery risk of at least 25.1432%, while a receiver retaining a seven-dimensional quantum memory achieves at most 24.2100%: a certified improvement of at least 0.9332 percentage points. In the strict counted-register model, the smallest separating memory dimension is four; certified separations also extend to three and four rounds.

Entanglement Budgets and Certified Energy Floors for Variational Quantum Circuits

Submitted to · Physical Review A.
Shreya Savadatti. Preprint on Zenodo.

I derive pre-training, optimizer-independent lower bounds on the energy error of a fixed variational quantum eigensolver circuit, so the circuit’s limits can be certified before any training. Across a cut of the qubit register, the entangling gates crossing that cut limit the Schmidt rank the circuit can reach; comparing that limit with the Schmidt spectrum of the target and multiplying by the spectral gap gives a closed-form rank floor, which I evaluate without truncation to 128 qubits. Gate counting alone is often too loose: in the Heisenberg ensemble the trained error is only 13–19× above the certified floor at budgets of seven to nine gates, and placing gates to minimize the floor does not reliably lower trained energies. To capture continuous entangling strength I associate each crossing gate with its periodic reduced angle, giving an angle-budget certificate that contains the rank floor, is tight for a single crossing entangler, and is numerically saturated in controlled rank-two tests. A continuous-time version gives a pulse-area and interaction-time bound, and when the circuit preserves a symmetry of the Hamiltonian the floor strengthens by orders of magnitude in the ordered transverse-field Ising regime. The certificates can rule out circuit families that cannot create enough cross-cut entanglement before training, though minimizing the rank-based floor does not by itself reliably improve circuit layouts.

Learning and Inverting the Error of a Trapped-Ion ZZ Gate

Supported by IonQ Research and IonQ research credits.
Shreya Savadatti. Write-up.

I developed a four-parameter noise model for the native trapped-ion ZZ gate and three short calibration-circuit families that learn dephasing, coherent rotation error, and depolarization without Pauli twirling. I then derived a first-order correction that adjusts measured observables without changing the original circuit. In five-qubit simulations, this reduced observable bias by 6–15× compared with uncorrected measurements. I then ran it on IonQ Forte. The calibration circuits showed that the ZZ gate at 0.1 turns over-rotates by about 4% per gate, with only about 0.2% incoherent error. On a five-qubit circuit with 32 ZZ gates, the correction halved the bias after readout mitigation (from −0.35 to −0.17), the same as zero-noise extrapolation, with a smaller error bar. About half of the error in that circuit is not explained by a model learned on one ion pair.

Current work: I am finding where the remaining error comes from by measuring each ion pair separately, checking for crosstalk onto neighbouring ions, measuring single-qubit gate error, and checking for drift between runs. I will then reanalyse the existing hardware data with the per-pair model.

Failure of Geometric Rényi Amortization Collapse Without Slot Assistance

Shreya Savadatti. Zenodo.

I study whether repeated, adaptive use of a quantum channel can ever be easier to distinguish than a single use, measured by the geometric Rényi divergence of superchannels. I show that this amortization collapse can fail when there is no reference system that bypasses the channel being tested. My counterexample uses commuting three-level states, a two-level output and output dephasing, and it gives a strict gap for every 1 < α ≤ 2. At α = 2 the unamortized divergence is exactly log2(3/2), about 0.585 bits, while an explicit rational witness reaches at least log2(183/116), about 0.658 bits. I also show that adding a three-dimensional bypass register removes the gap for this example, and I prove that collapse holds with a reference system for memoryless superchannel pairs. The general question for fully amortized network discrimination is still open.

Analysis of quantum fully homomorphic encryption schemes (QFHE) and hierarchial memory management for QFHE

Shreya Savadatti, Aswani Kumar Cherukuri, Annapurna Jonnalagadda, and Athanasios V. Vasilakos. Complex & Intelligent Systems, 11, 264 (2025). Review article.

In this review we compare the main approaches to quantum fully homomorphic encryption, which allows computation on encrypted quantum data, and look at how that encrypted data should be stored and moved during a computation. We then propose a hierarchical memory design with a quantum cache and a controller based on reinforcement learning. The paper links the security requirements of these schemes to practical questions about data placement, memory access and fault-tolerant computation. The memory design is a proposed framework and has not been evaluated on hardware.

Proof-Carrying Error Mitigation: Verifiable Error Bars for Quantum Cloud Results

Shreya Savadatti and Elias Towe. Based on my master's thesis.
Submitting to · IEEE Quantum Week (QCE 2027).

When a cloud quantum service returns an error-mitigated number, the user cannot see how it was produced. A software bug, an outdated calibration, or a dishonest provider could shift the answer, and mitigation tends to magnify such shifts. I wanted users to be able to check a mitigated result without trusting the provider. In this work, the provider returns each result with an error bar and a short certificate, and the user verifies the certificate on a laptop using exact arithmetic. I also proved which test circuits the certificate needs. Under independent readout errors, only Q+1 of the 2Q readout error directions can change a mitigated result, so Q+1 simple basis-state circuits cover everything that matters, while test circuits that imitate the target can miss up to 76% of it.

I tested the method in five runs on real hardware. On Rigetti Cepheus-1-108Q, the checks detected strong readout crosstalk (one qubit was misread 55% of the time when its neighbour was flipped), and the protocol refused to certify, as intended. On IBM ibm_fez, all four predictions I fixed before the run held. The error bars were up to 57% tighter, and a false calibration report that moved a GHZ result by 6.6 standard errors while passing every imitation test was caught. At 16 qubits, 17 circuits did the work of the 65,536 needed for full calibration. I also extended the certificate to zero-noise extrapolation. It covered the true value in every hardware run, but my own analysis showed that this depended on statistical slack and that a generic check circuit stopped tracking the target after IBM recalibrated the device.

Next: a check circuit built from each target's own gates, which sets the extrapolation bound from data. It is implemented and tested in simulation, and the hardware run is queued on IBM.

Physicality, Relaxation Gaps, and Resource Allocation in Pauli-Limited Quantum Certification

Shreya Savadatti and P. Y. Zhang. Manuscript in preparation.

With Peter Yun Zhang, I study how much gate fidelity can be certified from incomplete, noisy Pauli measurements. We solve the positivity-relaxed problem in terms of the target's stabilizer norm, then prove that this number alone does not determine the best physical certificate: an interval-arithmetic proof exhibits two two-qubit gates with exactly the same stabilizer norm but different certification robustness. We give an exact condition for when the relaxation is tight and derive shot-allocation rules for finite measurement budgets. The reproducibility package includes interval-checked examples for 200 two-qubit and 10 three-qubit targets and a simulated 81-setting measurement protocol. No hardware measurements are claimed.

Perpetual Fault Tolerance at Fixed Overhead for Contractive Quantum Filters and Recurrent Instruments

Shreya Savadatti. Manuscript.

I develop a fault-tolerance analysis for quantum filters that run continuously and gradually forget earlier disturbances. Standard whole-circuit error bounds grow with the number of operations; here, combining concatenated error correction with trace-distance contraction gives a bound on the current state and any fixed window of recent outputs that holds for every number of rounds, using a fixed encoding level with respect to runtime. The manuscript constructs a Steane [[7,1,3]] fault-tolerant implementation, uses machine-checked integer calculations for its stated gadget obligations, and derives an exact two-cycle contraction factor of 1/√2 for a driven weak-measurement example. The guarantee depends on the filter's contraction and fault-model assumptions; it does not cover the full growing output transcript.

Quantum Algorithms for Oscillatory Porous Flow: Preconditioning and Observable Estimation

Shreya Savadatti and Franz Franchetti. Manuscript in preparation.

With Franz Franchetti, I study quantum algorithms for estimating energy dissipation in oscillatory porous flow without recovering the full velocity field. We use a divergence-free Fourier representation and a frequency-aware preconditioner whose condition-number bound is independent of spatial grid resolution and approaches one at high frequency. For material parameters accessed by point queries, we develop a classical coarse approximation followed by quantum estimation of the correction. Under explicit smoothness and access assumptions, this gives a query upper bound of Õ(ε−d/(r+d)), with a matching quantum query lower bound in the manuscript's stated regime. Numerical checks support the operator identities; an end-to-end quantum speedup or hardware demonstration is not claimed.

QSIG: a state-informed generative quantum eigensolver

Finalist · Aqora quantum hackathon, Mitsubishi Chemical and AIST track.

I used state information, rather than energy alone, to select circuits for a generative eigensolver. In an eight-qubit benchmark, the selected circuit used 8 CZ gates instead of 16 and matched the baseline's noiseless energy to within 0.0117 mHa. Hardware testing told a different story: across 12 completed Rigetti jobs, the shallower circuit measured 105.56 mHa worse after postselection (95% confidence interval: 54.6–156.2 mHa). I report both the compilation saving and the negative hardware result.

ESS: Entropic Scenario Superposition

Finalist · Aqora quantum hackathon.

I modeled power-grid upgrades using a temperature parameter to vary risk preference without changing the value assigned to lost load. In a four-microgrid, 360-scenario test, worst-case unserved load fell from 15.66% to 5.57% as selected spending rose from $0 to $90,000. A degree-five polynomial formulation avoids 252,720 degree-reduction auxiliary qubits required by the corresponding QUBO representation. This is a representation comparison, not a quantum speedup: no Dirac-3 run was completed, and a smaller hardware-feasible approximation failed to preserve the exact optimum.

Quantum Machine Learning System for Chronic Kidney Disease Detection

Patent pending · Indian Patent Office, through VIT's IPR and Technology Transfer Cell.
Inventors: Kathiravan Srinivasan and Shreya Savadatti.

A hybrid quantum-classical classifier for early detection of chronic kidney disease from routine clinical data. LASSO selects the informative clinical features, and a sparse autoencoder generates synthetic minority-class samples to correct the class imbalance typical of medical datasets. The selected features are encoded into quantum states through a custom feature map with polynomial single-qubit rotations and controlled entanglement, and the resulting quantum kernel, computed from simulated statevectors, trains a support vector machine. The pipeline was benchmarked against classical models and standard Pauli and ZZ quantum feature maps.

Carnegie Mellon University, Information Networking Institute. In progress, expected May 2027.

My master's thesis develops checkable certificates for error-mitigated results from cloud quantum hardware.
