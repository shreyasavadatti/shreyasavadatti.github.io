I'm a master's student at Carnegie Mellon University and a Quantum Research Assistant in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering. My research is in quantum error correction, quantum information theory, and quantum security.

The question I keep coming back to is how you can trust a computer built entirely from noisy parts. I like problems where the answer can be proved, so a lot of my work ends in a theorem or a certificate: what an error-corrected memory can and cannot do, or how much a set of noisy measurements really tells you about a gate. I also run my ideas on real hardware, and some of my favorite results are the ones where the device disagreed with me and I had to work out why.

I'm especially drawn to finding where quantum methods truly outperform the strongest classical ones. A quantum advantage only counts if it survives the best classical competitor, so I look for separations that can be proved, like a quantum memory that beats every adaptive classical strategy for reading out error-correction data.

Since June 2026 I have also been a quantum computing intern at the U.S. Department of Energy's National Laboratory of the Rockies. I'm applying to PhD programs in quantum computing for Fall 2027, and I'm always glad to talk about research.

Failure of Geometric Rényi Amortization Collapse Without Slot Assistance

Shreya Savadatti. Zenodo.

I study amortization of geometric Rényi divergence for superchannels when an inserted channel has no reference assistance. A counterexample shows that collapse fails for every 1 < α ≤ 2 in this restricted setting. This does not establish failure for reference-assisted divergences.

Physicality, Relaxation Gaps, and Resource Allocation in Pauli-Limited Quantum Certification

Shreya Savadatti and P. Y. Zhang. In preparation for arXiv.

I study how requiring an unknown quantum channel to be physical changes what can be certified from noisy Pauli measurements. A computer-assisted interval proof gives two two-qubit gates with the same stabilizer norm but different certification robustness. The repository also contains interval-checked examples for 200 two-qubit and 10 three-qubit targets, plus a simulated measurement protocol using 81 Pauli-basis settings. No physical-device measurements are claimed.

Certified Separation Between Coherent and Adaptive Temporal Processing of Quantum-Error-Correction Readout

Shreya Savadatti. Manuscript.

I compare two ways of processing repeated quantum-error-correction readout: measuring each round with classical feed-forward, or retaining a small quantum memory between rounds. In a two-round model, a bound covering all separate adaptive receivers gives decision risk at least 25.1432%. A coherent receiver with a seven-dimensional memory reaches at most 24.2100%, a certified improvement of at least 0.9332 percentage points. The comparison uses the same signal ensemble and decision loss for both receiver classes.

Perpetual Fault Tolerance at Fixed Overhead for Contractive Quantum Filters and Recurrent Instruments

Shreya Savadatti. Manuscript.

This manuscript studies recurrent quantum filters that contract trace distance, so the influence of earlier errors diminishes over time. Under explicit contraction and fault-tolerance assumptions, the analysis combines concatenated level reduction with error contraction to obtain an error bound that does not grow with the number of rounds. It is not a general fixed-overhead claim for arbitrary quantum computations.

Entanglement Budgets and Certified Energy Floors for Variational Quantum Circuits

Shreya Savadatti. Manuscript.

I derive bounds on the energies reachable by variational quantum circuits with limited entangling resources. The bounds depend on the gates and entangling angles across circuit cuts, and apply before any optimization is run. They distinguish a circuit's inability to represent a target state from an optimizer's failure to find it.

Shreya Savadatti. Manuscript.

I analyze quantum linear-system methods for oscillatory flow through porous media. The manuscript develops a preconditioner with a grid-independent condition-number bound under stated assumptions, and query bounds for estimating dissipation-related observables. These are conditional algorithmic bounds, not an unconditional claim of speedup over classical solvers.

QSIG: a state-informed generative quantum eigensolver

Finalist · Aqora quantum hackathon, Mitsubishi Chemical and AIST track.

I used state information, rather than energy alone, to select circuits for a generative eigensolver. In an eight-qubit benchmark, the selected circuit used 8 CZ gates instead of 16 and matched the baseline's noiseless energy to within 0.0117 mHa. Hardware testing told a different story: across 12 completed Rigetti jobs, the shallower circuit measured 105.56 mHa worse after postselection (95% confidence interval: 54.6–156.2 mHa). I report both the compilation saving and the negative hardware result.

ESS: Entropic Scenario Superposition

Finalist · Aqora quantum hackathon.

I modeled power-grid upgrades using a temperature parameter to vary risk preference without changing the value assigned to lost load. In a four-microgrid, 360-scenario test, worst-case unserved load fell from 15.66% to 5.57% as selected spending rose from $0 to $90,000. A degree-five polynomial formulation avoids 252,720 degree-reduction auxiliary qubits required by the corresponding QUBO representation. This is a representation comparison, not a quantum speedup: no Dirac-3 run was completed, and a smaller hardware-feasible approximation failed to preserve the exact optimum.
